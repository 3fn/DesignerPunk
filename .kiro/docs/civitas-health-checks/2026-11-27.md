## 9. The volatile-path incident (2026-11-27): classified

**Decision: an issue record**, filed in this PR as `.kiro/issues/2026-11-27-committed-records-cite-volatile-paths.md` (Thurgood, P2 (record) · defect, `release:next:phase-1`).
- **Not this record alone**: rule 10 makes an obligation stated only in a walk table a finding.
- **Not a Spec 131 inbound**: 131 is parked behind 132's first family, so an inbound would leave the obligation unowned for months. 131 is named in the record as consulted for the eventual rule.

**The class**: a committed record names a location outside git as where its evidence lives. Two kinds are volatile:
- the session scratch folder, which macOS clears at restart;
- the local transcripts (`~/.claude/projects/…`), which exist on one machine only and are deleted after `cleanupPeriodDays` (180 here). The signing chain's `--audit` reads them.

**The measure**: at `3a3bd072b`, 88 files cite scratch paths (395 lines), and 56 cite transcripts. Most are provenance. The defect is the subset that relies on the folder for its evidence.

**Class option**: a rule that evidence a record relies on is committed by the PR that records it, with volatile paths allowed only as marked provenance. A warn-only check over added record lines would flag unmarked volatile paths from today's baseline.
- **Cost**: one ballot item (law), plus a small check whose home needs either a grant or a contract change.
- **Surviving counter-argument**: it adds law to a corpus that Spec 131 exists to shrink. The ballots already splice consult files byte for byte, and that habit lost nothing; the losses came where the habit was not followed.
