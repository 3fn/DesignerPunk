# .kiro/issues/ — the active-charter convention

**Established**: 2026-09-19 (the issues-dir triage — chartered as the load-bearing precondition of the monthly health check's active-charter walk; Spec 127 outline-settle ballot § 5.5, Peter's tracking ruling; owner Thurgood)
**The rule in one line**: **anything in this directory's root is ACTIVE; everything closed lives in `archive/`.** The walk reads every root record (rule 6); rule 5 keeps the root bounded — it is not bounded by construction.

---

## The convention

1. **Root = active.** A file at the root is a live charter, an open defect, or a deferred item — and it SHALL carry an **owner** and a **named trigger** (an event, never a calendar hope). The trigger line begins `**Trigger**: ` followed immediately by exactly one backticked token naming **the event that owes the close** — `tag:v<semver>`, `merge:#<N>`, `path:<glob>` or `path:<glob>,<glob>,…`, `release:<ver>:phase-1` or `manual` — then optional prose; a start condition, if any, is prose (definitions: ballot 2026-10-18-obligation-drain-and-release-falsity-floor M2). A `path:` list fires as one glob does, on a touch of any glob in it; no space stands beside a comma *(ballot 2026-10-26-issues-multi-path-trigger-token)*. A trigger names the event that owes the close, never a vehicle the work hopes to ride; a P0, P1 or P2 record that would ride another change takes `release:next:phase-1`, or `merge:#<N>` once the vehicle PR exists, and a P3 that would ride takes `manual`, naming its own fixing PR as the event *(ballot 2026-10-22-trigger-names-the-event-not-the-vehicle)*. Only the record's owner writes or changes its Trigger line (rule 9). A **P1 consumer-blocking** record (rule 12) may not carry `manual`: it takes a git-visible token where one expresses the event, and otherwise `release:next:phase-1`, with the start condition in prose (ballot 2026-10-20-manual-records-routed-readers F-3 (a′)). A root file without both is itself a walk finding.
2. **`archive/` = closed records.** Resolved defects, executed charters/session briefs, superseded notes, canonical adjudication records whose rulings are law elsewhere, stale investigation notes, and accidental duplicates. Files move by `git mv`, content UNCHANGED — the record is immutable; the move is the only edit.
3. **Citation resolution**: historical records (ballots, completion docs, register history entries) cite `.kiro/issues/<file>` paths as written at their time — those citations are point-in-time and are NOT rewritten when a file archives. **Resolution rule: a cited issue path not found at the root is in `archive/` under the same filename.** (One flat directory, no renames, so resolution is mechanical.)
4. **Resurrection**: an archived-stale item that turns out to be live moves BACK to the root by `git mv`, gaining an owner + trigger in the same commit. Archiving is never a ruling on the merits — the merits live in the file.
5. **Closing an item**: when a root item resolves/executes, record the outcome IN the file (dated), then `git mv` it to `archive/` — ideally in the PR that closes it, otherwise at the next health-check walk. A record whose outcome is recorded is archived no later than the next walk; a grant archives at its fixing PR's merge; a P3 with a `manual` trigger still unfired at two consecutive walks is archived stale, its move commit adding the dated outcome line "archived stale — no outcome" (rule 4 brings it back).
6. **The walk** (monthly health check, Thurgood) is a triage sitting, recorded in the health-check record: for each root record — has its trigger fired (rule 1)? If fired, it is closed and archived, closed as done-but-unclosed (no lapse), re-ruled by its owner, or lapsed (rule 11). The headline prints, per class: records, fired-open, lapses, and `manual` (not computable) of the total; the escalations owed to Peter go to him as one batch. A root record missing owner, trigger token or class = finding.
7. **Instrument-surfaced rows** (register entry `instrument-rows-disposed-not-fixed`; ballot 2026-09-27-ci-regime-standing-scope § 4): an instrument's PR carries no fix outside its own extent; each surfaced row is, in that PR, adjudicated under the instrument's `sweep:` key or filed here as a chartered item (owner + named trigger + a unique expiry string in the adjudication `record`, removed by the fix PR with `grep -c` → 0). A surfaced row with neither disposition is a walk finding.
8. **Grant paths** (ballot 2026-09-27-ci-regime-standing-scope § 3): an issue whose body carries a `**Grant paths**:` list grants its named owner write scope over exactly those paths, on the fixing PR's branch, until that PR merges — activated by Peter's merge of a PR whose body names the grant; the fixing PR is diffed against the list as it stood at that merge; a `.github/**` grant carries M1's excluded acts unless named and admitted; never a governance-law path; the fixing PR cites its path list. A `.github/**` grant's activating PR carries, in its own body, the line `**ARMING**: at this merge and at the fixing PR's merge`; a grant whose fixing PR will delete a line on a CI path quotes it in a `**Deleted lines**:` block (Stacy's ARMING row). An out-of-list edit is a claims-pass finding. A C1 signing or confirming act needs no grant (ballot 2026-10-01-signing-act-chain; register `signing-act-consistency`).
9. **Who writes an issue file** (ballot 2026-10-17-standing-write-scope-six-roots § 2.5): the seat(s) named in a record's `**Owner**:` or `**Owners**:` header author and edit its body; a section jointly authored by co-owners is edited by any of them, and a line signed by one seat is rewritten only by that seat. Any seat may append a dated line signed with its name to any record. Thurgood, as the convention's steward, walks every record, appends walk lines, and closes and archives records under rule 5. An edit outside this rule is a claims-pass finding.
10. **Filing rule.** A ruling or a claims pass that creates an obligation files an issue record for it, or appends to the existing one, **in the same PR that records the ruling or the pass**. An obligation stated only in a plan line, a carry list, a walk table, a rulings row or a PR body, citing no record, is a finding on the PR that wrote it. A record filed for another owner names its filer; rule 9 applies to every later edit. A grant list is never authored only inside the diff it bounds: it is in a merged record first, or Peter's direction naming its paths is on the record **outside** that diff — a dated line in a record merged earlier, or Peter's own comment on the PR. An observation record that a CORRECTION cites (`.kiro/docs/ballots/README.md` § "When a governance edit needs a ballot — three kinds") may be merged before the CORRECTION or travel in its PR, provided it resolves at the CORRECTION's merge commit `<m>`: `git cat-file -e <m>:<the path as cited>` succeeds *(added 2026-10-29 by ballot 2026-10-26-release-flow-batch-16-1, RS-43)*.
11. **Lapse and escalation.** A record **lapses** at a firing event (rule 1) when no commit that is an ancestor of the event's commit closed it (rule 5's dated outcome line, or the archive move) or re-ruled it (its Trigger line changed in a diff carrying a dated reason and authority). **A record still open after a lapse lapses again at each later release's S.** **A record filed after the release's walk record merged** — its adding commit is not an ancestor of that merge — whose Trigger token is `release:next:phase-1` does not lapse at that release's S if the walk record has a row for it at S; its first firing is then the next release's S. A record filed then and not rowed lapses at S like any other. A row is a line of the walk record that begins `|` and contains the record's file name (`<date>-<slug>.md`, with or without a `.kiro/issues/` prefix). Records whose token is `release:<V>:phase-1` or `tag:v<V>` are not carried. The release falsity floor is unchanged (RELEASE-FLOW 3a) *(added 2026-10-26 by ballot 2026-10-25-rule-11-post-walk-filings-and-spec-answers, sentence 1)*. Whoever observes a lapse — the walker, the walk's author, a claims pass, an owner at session start (Start Up Tasks item 9) — appends `## Lapse (YYYY-MM-DD)` naming the firing commit's SHA, signed. The headings are append-only, a later re-ruling never erases one, and **the count is the number of distinct SHAs they name**. A fixing PR closes its record by adding rule 5's outcome line in the same PR. **Escalation**: at the **second** lapse of a P1 or a P2 `(record)` record, or the **first** of a P0 or a P2 `(guard)` record, the record goes to Peter with exactly three options — **spec**, **drop**, or **fix-now** (a git-visible trigger no later than the next release's S). A lapsed fix-now returns without that option. A **spec** answer is carried out when the spec's design outline merges and names the record. From that merge the record's owner re-rules its Trigger to the event that owes its close (the record's own deadline, or the spec's fixing PR once it exists), and the record closes under rule 5 when its items close. Until the outline merges, each later lapse returns the record to Peter with his spec answer quoted *(added 2026-10-26 by ballot 2026-10-25-rule-11-post-walk-filings-and-spec-answers, sentence 2)*. Grants are excluded; a grant still open at its fixing PR's merge is a walk finding. A charter cannot lapse before its trigger fires.
12. **Class.** Every root record carries `**Class**: <level> <name> · <kind>`. Levels: **P0 false-claim** (the release falsity floor's definition — ballot 2026-10-18-obligation-drain-and-release-falsity-floor M7), **P1 consumer-blocking**, **P2 obligation** with **exactly one, mandatory** sub-tag — **`(guard)`** (a guard or instrument weaker than its claim) or **`(record)`** (the record or its form is wrong; no reader is misled) — and **P3 improvement**. Kinds: `defect`, `owed-act`, `charter`, `grant`. The owner classes; a dated line re-classes a record when what it describes ships. Only Peter downgrades a P0. Stacy may confirm or dispute a P0 reading, and may read a non-P0 record as P0 — a finding to Peter, never an upgrade by her (Q5; her read, never a gate).
