## 3. Where the two readings come from

- **Peter's words (R9, item 1)**: "**root records go down across 16.2.0, not up.** That's the measure that the cleanup is cleaning." That is in the rulings record, L115–L119; § "What his words cover" names that bullet at L130. "Across 16.2.0" does not name a window.
- **The brief's narrowing**: the 16.2.0 release brief turned his bullet into criterion 4, "The root count at S is below the count at the walk's start, with both stated in the walk record" (rulings record L155, in the brief's text, L150–L157). The window became the walk start to S. The rulings record's own reading (L138–L148) says his words covered the offer as shown, not the brief's wording of it. That is why the window was put to him at S.
- **What the two readings disagree about**: the records filed between 16.1.0's S and the walk start. In that span, 25 root records were added and 4 archived, a net +21 (`comm` of the two `git ls-tree` lists). The cleanup reading leaves them out. The release-to-release reading counts them.

## 4. The input

- **The baseline record**: `.kiro/specs/131-governance-simplification/evidence/2026-11-04-baseline-16.2.0.md` (added at `f5ebacd88`, on the post-S records branch). It is generated by `baseline-snapshot.sh` for both releases, with every figure beside its command. It includes root-record words, ballots, grants, archive moves by outcome kind, and product share.
- **Two things the measure has to settle, from that record**: whether archiving a routed record counts (fork B ruled that routing makes no archive move, R11 line 14), and whether the window starts at the previous release's S or at the walk. The record's unclassified archive moves (37 of 41) are the script's heuristic, not a finding; a measure that counts outcomes needs one outcome grammar.

## 5. Counter-argument, surviving

A measure fixed in advance can be met by moving records instead of resolving them. The release-to-release reading would have been beaten here too, by archiving the 28 P3s instead of routing them, and fork D ruled against that. So the measure 131 defines should count resolutions, not root-count deltas alone. Peter's words, "the measure that the cleanup is cleaning", point that way.
